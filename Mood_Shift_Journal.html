Mood Shift Journal

A guided mood-shifting tool designed to help you understand and manage upsetting emotions by examining your thoughts and developing more balanced perspectives using Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) techniques. This independently developed educational journal is based on general CBT principles and inspired in part by Dr. David D. Burns’ Daily Mood Journal. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by Dr. Burns or Feeling Good. Your entries are stored locally in your browser and are not sent to a server by this tool. You may print or save your journal as a PDF.

Step 1: Describe the Upsetting Situation in Brief

Briefly describe one specific upsetting situation. Include what happened, without making assumptions about other people's intentions.

Step 2: Identify Your Feelings, Moods, or Emotions — Select and Rate Them

Select the feelings you noticed. For each selected feeling, estimate its intensity on a personal 0–10 scale (0 = absent, 10 = extremely strong). These are subjective ratings, not clinical measurements.

Step 3: Capture the Automatic or Upsetting Thoughts Behind the Situation and Feelings

Write the automatic or upsetting thoughts that came to mind in the situation and contributed to the feelings selected in Step 2. Record each thought separately.

Step 4: Capture Core Values and Benefits of Feelings and Thoughts Through Positive Reframing

Write down the core values or potential benefits reflected by your selected feelings and thoughts.

Step 5: Set Healthy Intensity That Retains the Core Values and Benefits

Use the initial intensity from Step 2 as a read-only reference. Set a healthy intensity for each feeling based on your personal situation and the core values identified in Step 4. These values are personal goals, not universal medical standards.

Step 6: Find Thought Distortions in the Upsetting Thoughts

For each thought recorded in Step 3, select any thinking patterns that may be relevant. These are descriptive CBT concepts, not diagnoses.

Step 7: Write a More Realistic Alternative Thought

For each upsetting thought, write a more realistic alternative thought that (1) substantially reduces your belief in the original upsetting thought and (2) is something you strongly believe. You can use the listed techniques to develop your alternative thought. Select the technique or techniques you used, then rate how strongly you believe the alternative.

Step 8: Reassess Your Belief in the Upsetting Thought

Review the original upsetting thought from Step 3 and record how strongly you believe it now. Initial beliefs are shown as read-only references.

Step 9: Reassess Your Emotional Intensity

Review your initial intensities from Step 2 and the healthy intensities from Step 5. Enter how strongly you feel each emotion now.

Step 10: Mood Shift Check

Reflect on your updated beliefs and feelings. There is no required outcome, and it is okay if a change takes more time.
